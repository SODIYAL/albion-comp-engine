A role checklist asks

“Do we have a healer?”

That hides hybrids, assumes every healer is interchangeable, and cannot explain why one fits this party better than another.

The capability model asks

“How much sustain, burst recovery and safety do we have?”

A role emerges from the weapon’s actual functions. The engine can see partial coverage, overlap and multi-purpose picks.

05
Calculation

Marginal gain

The engine sums the party’s capability supply — each member counted with the one-spell-per-slot kit they would actually run — values the first useful units more than redundant ones, protects critical floors, penalizes overstacking and exact duplicates, then tries every candidate weapon. Forging a comp runs a deterministic beam search over complete rosters under expert composition rules (healer and frontline counts, copy limits, a ranged AoE core for big fights, size-band weapon viability), then polishes with one- and two-slot swap passes. A slot the objective dislikes but a rule demands — or one nothing can improve — is flagged, never hidden.

candidate score =
0.55 × Δfitness + 0.20 × Δsynergy
+ 0.15 × meta guard + viability − duplicates
deterministicPython ↔ browser parity

Mechanics used today

Simple rules that model real composition pressure.

These controls prevent the engine from blindly stacking the largest raw numbers.

Σ

Capability supply

Every party member adds 0–3 units for each function their kit can provide. Roles are labels for display, not scoring inputs.

n↗

Adaptive targets

Healing, frontline and damage needs grow with party size; threshold utilities such as cleanse can stay nearly flat.

γ.7

Diminishing returns

A concave utility curve makes the first missing unit worth more than another copy of something already covered.

!

Hard floors

Critical failures such as zero healing get an extra penalty. This keeps “broadly useful” weapons from beating a load-bearing need.

Q/W

One spell per slot

A weapon’s sheet lists every spell it can run, but a player equips one Q, one W, one E and one passive. Each party member is scored on the single best loadout it can actually field — never two alternatives of the same slot at once — so a weapon with a wide spell menu no longer counts as if it brought all of them.

CAP

Overstack penalties

Supply beyond a template’s soft cap earns less and less, exposing redundant composition choices. The penalty saturates, so redundancy always costs something but never makes a covered capability worth less than nothing — a full 20 can’t score below a half-empty one.

A×B

Capability synergies

Pairs such as clump creation + AoE burst or resist shred + single-target burst earn value across players: a pair only counts when the content actually asks for both halves, and one weapon supplying both sides of a pair cannot trigger it alone — someone has to shred for someone else's burst.

STYLE

Playstyle weights

Brawl, clap, kite and brawl-clap change the value of capabilities while the balanced style leaves the content template untouched.

δ

Priors, duplicates

A tiny hand-set meta guard plus an expert viability tier (weapons real callers field at this size) nudge scores; neither is live win-rate data and both are deliberately weaker than capability gain. Exact duplicate weapons pay a growing per-copy cost — the third copy of a staff shares cooldown windows with the first two — except where real comps prove the stack (two Permafrosts, three Hallowfalls).

fitness = Σ useful coverage (+ a small headroom band) − floor gaps − overstack
pick score = .55Δfitness + .20Δsynergy + .15meta guard + viability − duplicates

Plain English: try a weapon with the one-spell-per-slot kit it would actually run, measure how much healthier the whole composition becomes, then rank the change. The reported score is the comp-score change — the same number you would see by adding the weapon and subtracting.

Combat physics · wired

Albion’s Focus Fire / Resilience and AoE Escalation tables now adjust effective single-target and AoE supply by playstyle. The curves are normalized to balanced, and Python/browser parity plus three golden checks pin their direction. The per-style “targets hit” assumptions are still provisional; Disarray is recorded but deliberately does not alter mirror-fight composition scoring.

How the flow is verified

Four different questions need four different proofs.

“The code agrees with itself” is useful, but it is not the same as “the recommendations are good.” The project tracks both.

Passed · data integrity
137/137

Evidence lint

Every nonzero score cites a spell the weapon can equip. The lint also checks that the effect can support the claim and that self, ally and enemy direction is correct.

Passed · intended behavior
18/18

Golden cases

Permanent scenarios assert basics such as “a damage-only party needs healing,” then pin style and combat-mechanics directions as the model grows.

Passed · implementation parity
60/60

Python equals browser

Random parties across every template and style match to 1e-9. A build-time fixture also checks the browser against Python on every page load.

Pending · recommendation quality
≥70%

Expert blind test

Three or more experienced shotcallers independently choose the next pick. The gate is their choice appearing in the engine’s top three at least 70% of the time.

Current accuracy evidence

A first weak-form leave-one-out baseline reached 69% role-level agreement, but it used two comps that also helped calibrate the templates. That makes it a useful diagnostic, not independent validation. The page therefore says pre-validation.

The honest boundary

What the engine does not know yet.

These are product limits, not hidden assumptions. Several become the roadmap below.

×

No armor or consumables in scoring

Helmets, chest pieces, shoes, capes, food and potions are not capability sheets yet.

×

No observed active spells

Killboards expose gear but not the chosen Q/W abilities, so optional kit capabilities remain conditional.

×

No player skill, IP or cost

A mechanically good pick may still be hard to pilot, expensive or wrong for a specific player.

×

No enemy-comp input

The templates model the expected field. Purge and anti-dive cannot yet respond to the exact opposing roster.

×

No learned win-rate signal

Killboard frequency is shown for context but cannot influence recommendations before the statistical gates pass.

×

No independent accuracy claim

Mechanical evidence is complete; the expert blind test remains the real recommendation-quality gate.

Roadmap

Improve trust first, then add intelligence.

The order matters: validate the recommendations before adding more knobs or learning from noisier data.

1
Now · quality gate

Independent shotcaller blind test

Send the same hidden-output scenarios to 3+ experienced callers. Score top-three agreement and turn every correct expert disagreement into a permanent regression test.

EXIT GATE · ≥70% top-three agreement
2
Next · calibration

Expert correction and saturation pass

Review all proposed capability scores, provisional content templates and style multipliers. Address the observed support undervaluation and breadth-over-depth behavior at full-party saturation.

DELIVERABLE · expert-reviewed dataset and templates
3
Combat physics

Refine the mechanics layer

Get expert sign-off on the wired style assumptions, then add per-weapon Resilience Penetration, CC escalation and per-spell escalation eligibility where the game data supports them—without double-counting existing utility curves.

DELIVERABLE · higher-fidelity scale mechanics
4
Build fidelity

Add equipment and kit variants

Give armor, boots, capes and consumables the same evidence-cited treatment. Represent weapon × kit archetypes so optional Q/W choices no longer look guaranteed.

DELIVERABLE · full-build capability model
5
Learning layer

Promote battle data carefully

Validate content labeling, compute win lift with minimum sample sizes and Bayesian shrinkage, and let statistics propose—never silently apply—template or meta-prior changes.

DELIVERABLE · human-approved, data-informed updates
6
Product depth

Model the actual matchup

Add expected enemy composition, player comfort, cost and difficulty filters; improve multi-slot lookahead so the forge plans a coherent remaining roster instead of only the best immediate pick.

DELIVERABLE · recommendations shaped to the real team

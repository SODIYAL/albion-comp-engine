## 2026-09-07 — Cost gate retired

**Context.** The cost gate had been added because the engine kept putting
the crystal holy staff (Exalted) in every comp for its area cleanse. The
better reading: that type of cleanse is not as important in small groups as
the engine valued it — mechanics, not weapon restrictions. Measured before
changing anything: the Exalted Staff's sheet carries no `cleanse` at all —
it is the catalogue's ONLY `anti_zone` supplier (Holy Dispel removes enemy
ground areas), so with the gate lifted the forge took it in 8 of 8
contents, 7-man to 25-man, on an `anti_zone` target every template carried
as `scales: false`. Evidence for the physics: no 7-man comp in the corpus
fields an Exalted (push_monkey_7, sob_blaze_os, sortasaucy_7man); the
curated comps that do are 20-mans, one 14 and one 10; the harvest (killer
parties) fields one in 2.0% of 4-9 man parties, 7.2% at 10-14, 21.5% at
15-19, 32.1% at 20+.

**Decision.** The cost gate is removed; weapons are not restricted, the
mechanics carry the judgment.

**Changes** (no weapon list): the gate removed from both ports and
`composition.yaml` (`cost_tier` stays a display fact; `off_budget` gone
from swap review and the page); `anti_zone` rows DELETED at castle_outpost
and roads ("never invent a number" — the 2.02 target there was a fossil of
the pre-08-27 anti_zone meaning, supported by no small comp) and set to
`scales: true` at blackzone_roam / territory_defense / castle / faction_war
(zone removal is demand created by enemy ground effects; more enemies lay
more; the base-size number stays what the vetted comps field).

**Evidence.** No Exalted in any default 7-man forge (castle_outpost, roads,
blackzone 7); Exalted still generated from 10 up (blackzone 10 target 0.9);
other crystal weapons compete on their merits (Arclight Blasters at 7, Rift
Glaive at 20 clap). Open: whether 10-14 should field it (7% of winners do,
one curated 10-man does) — the lever is the base-20 measurement the scaling
reads, never a weapon rule (resolved by the ramp below). T42 + F14 pin it;
T27 keeps its E-identity half.

## 2026-09-07 — The anti_zone demand ramp

**Context.** The scaled row still fielded the Exalted Staff at 10. The
read: if only 7% of winners field it, the need should scale — not needed at
10-14, growing slightly with numbers, a real requirement at 25+.

**Changes.** A general template-row mechanism, `ramp: {none_until,
full_at}` in both ports: the row is DROPPED for the context at sizes ≤
none_until (identical to a content with no row — no target, no weight,
nothing to chase), grows linearly from zero to the row's measured value at
full_at, and grows with the party beyond it (the same proportional rule
`scales` uses); target and soft cap move together; a row carries `ramp` or
`scales`, never both. anti_zone rows at blackzone_roam / territory_defense /
castle / faction_war carry `ramp: {none_until: 14, full_at: 25}` — the
anchors are the stated sizes, the row values stay the comp-fitted numbers.

**Evidence.** No anti_zone requirement at 7, 10 or 14 (no Exalted forged);
target 0.16 at 15, 0.98 at 20 (blackzone), 2.21 at 20 (territory), 1.8 at
25 (castle), 2.16 at 30. Noted: from 15 up the forge fields the Exalted
Staff even on the 0.16 sliver — it is the sole supplier, so any nonzero row
makes it the tie-break among otherwise equal healers; the ramp sets the SIZE
of the need exactly as decided, and whether a hybrid healer should win a tie
on a sliver is a weight question, not a size one. T42 (7- and 10-man forges
field none; 25-man does; manual scores) and F14 (the ramp values at
14/20/25/30) pin it; parity 60/60.

**Standing rule** (moved here from the retired README so it outlives the
scaffold; standing rule 16), for any future coefficient tuning:

- TRAIN — may be inspected freely while changing the model. Every case ever
  discussed in a session, converted to a golden test, or used to motivate a
  change is train-contaminated and can never leave train. Golden tests are
  the regression floor and are all train by definition.
- VALIDATION — used to COMPARE candidate settings during a sweep. Aggregate
  metrics only; a validation case that gets individually debugged moves to
  train.
- HOLDOUT — never examined while tuning. Collected in fresh validation
  rounds, scored only when a tuning round is declared finished, reported
  separately. Holdout misses do not become golden tests until the
  evaluation round is complete. A holdout set that has been scored against
  and iterated on is spent — retire it to validation and collect a new one.
- The anti-circularity rule is the bridge: cases derived from comps that
  calibrated a template never drive retuning of that template's numbers.

Until validation and holdout sets exist, any parameter sweep is a
sensitivity map, not a calibration — no coefficient moves on train evidence
alone.

## 2026-09-11 — Slot controls: lock, replace, refresh the rest

**Context.** Request: beside the remove control on a hovered weapon, a
button to replace the current weapon with something else, a button to lock
the weapon, and a button to refresh the rest of the comp that is not
locked. Four design decisions: replace shows RANKED alternatives and the
user picks; lock is the ONLY thing a refresh holds (manual picks included);
refresh gives A DIFFERENT viable comp each press; the hovered weapon counts
as locked for its refresh.

**Engine** (both ports, parity 60/60 carrying both):

- `forge(..., avoid=[rosters])` returns the best roster whose weapon
  multiset is not among those shown: the final beam depth drops avoided
  completions, and `_refine_constrained` / `_two_opt` refuse a move that
  lands on one (a refined alternative used to be able to walk back into the
  previous answer). Deterministic — the list is the input, there is no
  seed. When every completion the final beam reaches is avoided the best of
  them comes back flagged `exhausted`. `roster_key()` is the multiset key
  (weapons only: a roster differing by a kit is the same comp to the
  caller). F32: three presses under the same locks give three distinct,
  feasible, non-improving rosters, every one on one healer (the typical
  gate holds on every alternative); the same avoid list replays the same
  roster; exhaustion reports.
- `replace_options(party, i, combos, gears, top_n)` — a one-slot forge:
  `_add_ok` + `_forge_eval_pick` at zero slots left against the REST of the
  roster, so every option passes role bands, typical counts, seat maxima,
  dup caps and the minima. Ranked by quantized score then weapon id;
  `delta` is the exact comp_score change of applying it (F33c at 1e-9).
  F33: replacing a dps in a one-healer 7-man never offers a healer;
  replacing the only full healer offers only full healers. The page's older
  "better options" hint runs the looser swap review and would have offered
  the second healer the same week's typical-count decision removed.

## 2026-09-30 — Guilds: platform phase 2

**Context.** Phase 1 left profiles and weapon lists readable by their own
account alone, and the spec's phase 2 sketch: `guilds`, `guild_members`
with four roles, guild-scoped reads, a guild panel. Two questions were
open: who inside a guild reads what, and how a member joins.

**Decision.** A guild lives on one server, its name unique there whatever
its case. Joining is by a 10-character code the guild carries: officers
and admins read it through a view, an admin renews it by writing any
value, which the guard replaces with a fresh code, so a code is never
chosen. `join_guild` runs as the caller: the code rides the statement as
a transaction-local setting and the insert policy checks it through
`private.guild_id_for_code`. `private.guild_role_of` is the policy helper
(a policy on `guild_members` cannot read the table under its own policy)
and answers for the caller alone. Every definer helper lives in the
schema `private`, which the API does not expose; the schema test lists
each and pins the schema.

them; admins set any role and remove anyone; a member leaves; the last
admin can neither step down nor leave by their own statement. Every
member reads co-members' character, display name, server and weapon
lists (curation judgment: the tool exists to show a caller what members
play); email addresses are never exposed. Bounds: 500 members per guild,
20 guilds per account, the guild name under the account name bound.

**Evidence.** The security advisor on the first migration: lint 0029 on
`guild_role_of` and `join_guild`, both definers signed-in users could call
through the API; the performance advisor: two foreign keys without an
index (lint 0001) and two permissive select policies on `profiles` and
`player_weapons` (lint 0006). The second migration (`guild_lints`) moved
the helper to `private`, made `join_guild` an invoker, added the indexes

then showed no finding from these tables. A row a statement inserts is
checked against the select policy before the helper's snapshot can see

own membership rows besides the guild's. The RLS suite's first run of the account-deletion case: the
cascade from `auth.users` through `profiles` to `guild_members` reached

showed the after-delete trigger at depth 1, so the succession keys off

## 2026-09-30 — Sign-up: platform phase 5

**Context.** Phase 4 gave a guild its CTAs with a share code. The spec's
phase 5: players claiming slots, guests possible, a claim that two
players cannot both win. The open question was guest identity, and
behind it how a guest, who has no session, reaches anything at all when
rule 1 grants `anon` nothing.

**Decision.** Guest identity is a name and a claim token: 16 random
bytes the browser makes on the first sign-up and keeps per CTA; the row
stores their SHA-256, the token rides the statement and is never stored;
that browser alone edits or cancels, and an account signing up from it
later adopts the row. A guest's reach is the share code carried by the
statement (rule 13): policies `to anon` on `events`, `event_slots`,
`guilds` and `signups` check it or delegate to the event's policy; the
grants to `anon` are the columns a guest sees and the sign-up a guest
writes; every guest function runs as the caller. One sign-up per player
per CTA, claimed in one statement (`ON CONFLICT` on the player's own
partial unique index; a claimed slot is the slot index's `23505`);
written while the CTA is open, cancelled until it is completed; the
name stored as declared. The sheet is one dialog for both kinds of
player, opened by the link once the stored session has been read, or
from the CTAs dialog by a DOM event.

**Evidence.** The RLS suite's section 11 (32 cases): a guest with the
code reads one CTA and nothing without it; the caller column and the
join code stay out of `anon`'s reach; the second claim on a slot is
`23505`; a guest cannot touch another guest's row; an account adopts the
guest row its token names and signs up fresh with a token that names
nobody; a locked CTA takes no sign-up and still lets a player cancel; a
completed CTA keeps its sheet. Two facts the first run taught: a
function's `WHERE` needs column privilege on the code column (policies
do not), so `anon` reads `share_code` too, which the policy already

## 2026-10-01 — CTA times and the import's shorthand

**Context.** Two reports from use. The CTA form took its start and mass
times in the viewer's local zone only, while a call time is announced in
UTC (the game's clock), so a caller converted by hand. And a caller's
sheet of twenty rows read eight names; of the rest, six needed a check
and six read as no match: "GA (Cleanse)", "Rotcaller (Support)",
"Occult (Disengage)" and "Witchwork (DPS)" carried a bracketed word the
matcher took as part of the name, "Golem" names the Earthrune Staff by
its E, and the sheet's Head, Chest and Boots columns were read as
players.

**Decision.** The time fields are typed in UTC (the default, remembered
per browser) or in the caller's own zone (`toZoneInput`,
`fromZoneInput`); switching the zone rewrites the fields and leaves the
instant, so nothing is marked unsaved. Under each field `zoneEcho` shows
its other reading. The database still stores the instant. On the sheet
the label names the reader's zone beside the local time and keeps the
UTC time, and `eventCountdown` reads to the minute, repainted every
thirty seconds while the sheet is open. In the import a bracketed word
before or after a name is the slot's label (`parseSlotText`): the role
where the sheet names none, else part of the note; a count or a tier in
brackets is still a count or nothing. The catalog carries each line's E
spell names (`build.py`), and a text whose every word belongs to one
line's E alone is read as that line (the `spell` tier, before a close
spelling; a role word is never read through it). Gear headers name an
ignored column. The role tag and the per-role counts stay the weapon's
primary seat (one role read): "Witchwork (DPS)" saves the Witchwork
Staff with the role label DPS; whether counts follow the label is left
to the BACKLOG.

## 2026-10-01 — The setup panel holds the settings; the identity is a headline

**Context.** Two reports from use. The masthead carried a second row
(playstyle, planned size, content, a size-notice chip, the party count,
the forge actions, the parity and dataset chips) above a setup panel that

**Decision.** The masthead is one line (brand, fitness, identity verdict,

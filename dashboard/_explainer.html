Albion Online composition engine

It does not ask “what role is missing?” It asks “what can this party actually do?”

Comp Forge represents weapons as capability bundles, compares the current party against the selected content and playstyle, resolves legal spell kits, then tests candidate weapons by the exact marginal improvement they add.

The recommendation is mechanical and explainable. Popularity, killboard prevalence, reference builds and other evidence can support the decision, but they do not silently rewrite the engine score.
The loop

Party → requirements → gaps → candidates → next pick.

The current product leads with the decision a caller needs: comp status, biggest need, best next pick, what that pick fixes, and what remains weak afterward.

01

Choose the fight

Content, roster size and playstyle determine which capabilities matter and how strongly they matter.

02

Read the party

Every selected weapon contributes capabilities through a legal one-spell-per-slot loadout rather than a fantasy “all spells at once” kit.

03

Find the gaps

Targets, hard floors, diminishing returns, overstacking and synergies reveal what is load-bearing, weak, covered or excessive.

04

Try candidates

Every allowed candidate is evaluated one slot ahead with its best legal kit. Duplicates, viability and contextual priors are applied.

05

Explain the winner

The UI shows the same capability deltas that produced the score, not a separate story invented after ranking.

Capabilities

Roles are labels. Capabilities are the scoring language.

A “tank” label is useful to a player, but the engine needs to know whether that weapon actually brings engage, peel, displacement, silence, purge, zone control, survivability and so on.

Trust model

Evidence is visible, typed, and kept separate from effectiveness claims.

Curated capability scores cite equippable spells. Reference builds carry provenance. Killboard usage is observational. These are different kinds of evidence and the product should say which is which.

LayerWhat it tells youWhat it does not claim
Capability evidenceWhy a weapon receives a non-zero capability score.That the weapon is meta or optimal.
CompEngine scoreWhich candidate most improves the modeled composition under the selected context.Guaranteed real-world win rate.
Reference buildsObserved or curated examples of how a weapon has been equipped.That one build is universally correct.
Killboard prevalenceHow often equipment appears in sampled fights of a similar broad fight-size bucket.Party membership, causality or effectiveness.
Expert blind testWhether experienced callers independently place the engine's recommendation among acceptable choices.Perfect agreement or solved meta.
